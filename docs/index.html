Method

Replay the assistant turn.

Factual QA teaches short domain answers. Replay keeps the adapted model close to its original behavior on the general prompts and response prefixes where that behavior was expressed.

  1. Generate once

    Run 9,600 general instructions through the initial model’s native chat template and save the sampled replies as exact tokens.

  2. Pair the inputs

    Train on one domain QA sequence and one saved assistant turn per pair. Reuse the replay buffer for another domain on the same checkpoint.

  3. Match the reference

    Learn domain answers by cross-entropy. Apply forward KL to match the frozen initial model’s full next-token distributions on response positions in replay.

Loss = domain cross-entropy + 0.25 × replay forward KL

Each loss term averages over the response tokens in a sequence before averaging over sequences.

Controlled comparisons

Where replay happens matters.

Applying the reference loss to the initial model’s assistant turns preserved instruction following better than applying it to domain answers or unprompted samples.

The model’s own answers retain more.

With the same prompts and token loss, replaying answers from the initial model resulted in 3.1 pp forgetting. Replaying the tested dataset’s answers gave 7.5 pp.

On the initial model’s answers, soft targets gave 2.4 pp forgetting at the same replay weight. See all controls ↗

Training guide ↗

Study scope. Three 3–4B-class families, two domains, 300 optimization steps, and shared General benchmark questions. Domain acquisition uses model judges without human calibration. The study leaves safety behavior, long-context use, and larger models open.
